## Cross-cutting facts that surface in multiple perspectives

These are factual findings the perspectives below all lean on. Listed once here so they aren't repeated five times.

1. **Five different "client" names, only one real, none of them yours.** The site invents practices: `Lumera Aesthetics` (home + product live-demo copy — home.jsx:337/341, product.jsx:61), `Coastline Aesthetic Group` / `Studio Vela` / `Northline Skin Co.` (sample-work tiles — home.jsx:453/460/467), and `Halcyon Med Spa` (pipeline visuals alt text — product.jsx:127/162). Your real deployed demos — Lumera, Sela, Devereaux — appear nowhere except Lumera. So the site presents *four* invented brands plus one half-wired real one.
2. **Three different prototype URLs, none branded to rivrsystems.com.** The modal opens `https://lumera-aesthetics.vercel.app/` (app.jsx:464). The product page draws a fake browser chrome reading `lumera-prototype.run.app` over a *static screenshot* (product.jsx:73). The book page embeds the raw Cloud Run URL `https://rivr-booking-914650990846.us-central1.run.app/` (book.jsx:13). Your live subdomains (`lumera.rivrsystems.com`, etc.) are referenced in zero places.
3. **Dead links presented as live.** Sample-work "See it live →" links are `href="#"` (home.jsx:544) directly under copy that says "Each tile is a real prototype. Click any one to open it in a popup and walk the first screen" (home.jsx:513). Footer Privacy/Terms/Cookies and Instagram/LinkedIn are all `href="#"` (app.jsx:376–386).
4. **Conflicting timelines.** Home/Product say "two to three weeks" (product.jsx:31, FAQ product.jsx:461). The book page agenda says "Ready in a week" (book.jsx:19). A prospect reading both will notice.
5. **"Both answer the phone" — but there's no phone number anywhere.** About headline (about.jsx:27) and Benefits ("Text the same number you'd text a friend," home.jsx:408) both promise phone/text access. The only contact on the entire site is `join@rivrsystems.com` (app.jsx:354, about.jsx:223) — an address that reads like a recruiting inbox, not a sales contact.
6. **Zero real social proof.** No client names you can call, no testimonials, no logos, no case-study numbers. The only stats are cited *industry* statistics (home.jsx:237–241, genuinely well-sourced), not RIVR results.
7. **No pricing, anywhere.** Not even a "starting at" or "we'll scope it on the call."
8. **Dev-mode React + in-browser Babel.** `react.development.js` + `react-dom.development.js` + `@babel/standalone` transforming JSX on every page load in the browser (index.html:16–18, repeated across all four HTML shells). This is a prototype runtime, not a production build.
9. **Mobile nav links vanish.** At ≤600px, `.nav-link { display: none; }` (styles.css:1220) with no hamburger replacement. On a phone you get the logo and the "Book a walkthrough" pill — you cannot reach Product or About.

## Perspective 1 — Founder / operator (Thor)

**Verdict.** This is genuinely well-built and it does *not* read as Lovable-default at first glance — the motion system is hand-rolled (custom cubic-bezier solver in app.jsx:9, scroll-progress and sticky-pin hooks, a real horizontal-scroll section), and the palette/typography is restrained. It's better animated than rivr-web. But it is not yet "considered editorial" — it's *competent SaaS with a tasteful skin*, and three things give the game away: the placeholder content (invented client names, dead links, no price, no proof), the swapped typography (Instrument Serif instead of your Cormorant), and a handful of AI-tells in the copy ("calm, branded booking experience," "no fluff" energy without the actual artifact). It would survive a 5-second glance from a prospect. It would not survive a competitor agency's creative director opening the footer.

**Top 3 things working**
1. **The motion is earned, not stock.** The easing matches framer-motion's expressive curve by hand (app.jsx:7–27), reveals are masked line-by-line (RevealLines), the pipeline is a real sticky-scroll sequence (product.jsx Pipeline). This is the part of Jonas's build worth keeping wholesale.
2. **The voice, where it commits, is your voice.** "No new platform. No staff retraining. No data migration." (home.jsx:87); "We follow your rules, we don't ask you to follow ours." (product.jsx:263); "the calendar they already open every morning." That's the no-fluff register you wanted.
3. **Palette discipline.** One accent (`#C9A875`, gold/walnut — styles.css:13), cream/near-black grounds, generous whitespace, no gradient soup. Tasteful.

**Top 5 things broken or weak (ranked)**
1. **It's a beautiful shell wrapped around placeholder content.** Coastline/Studio Vela/Northline/Halcyon are invented; "See it live" goes to `#`; "Lumera is a med spa we partnered with" (home.jsx:341) overstates a demo into a client relationship. Anyone who *clicks* finds the seams in ten seconds. This is the single biggest "side-project" tell, not the design.
2. **Typography drifted off-brand.** You speced Cormorant; this ships Instrument Serif (styles.css:19, with Cormorant only as a fallback). Instrument Serif is a 2023 Google-font darling — it's *everywhere* on AI-built sites right now. That alone reads "made this year with the current default serif."
3. **The custom cursor (app.jsx:396) is a portfolio-tier flex that fights the brand.** `mix-blend-mode: difference` dot-follower is a 2021 agency-demo cliché. On a med-spa B2B site it reads as "developer showing off," not "considered." It also does nothing for the 42-year-old on her iPhone (it's hidden on touch).
4. **Copy AI-tells.** "calm, branded booking experience" (home.jsx:87), "Built to grow your practice, not your tooling stack" (home.jsx:430 — the X-not-Y construction is the most common LLM rhythm there is), and the FAQ answers all have the same tidy three-beat cadence. Not damning, but a trained eye clocks it.
5. **No proof and no price = it reads as pre-revenue.** For *you* that's the truth, but the site doesn't compensate with the things that make a two-person shop credible (a named client, a number, a face on a call). The bios lean on Muay Thai and "near qualifying for the Olympics" (about.jsx:106) instead of a single shipped result.

**Recommendations**
- Swap Instrument Serif → Cormorant Garamond across the `--font-serif` token (styles.css:19) if you want the original brand. One-line change, high signal.
- Kill or heavily soften the custom cursor (app.jsx:396, styles.css:368–381). Editorial brands (Aesop, Goop) don't use trick cursors.
- Replace every invented brand with Lumera / Sela / Devereaux and wire the real subdomains (see SYNTHESIS).
- Decide what's true about Lumera and say exactly that. "A demo we built to show the three booking patterns" is honest and still strong. "A med spa we partnered with" is not, unless it is.

## Perspective 5 — Senior front-end engineer

**Verdict.** This is a high-craft *prototype* runtime shipped as production. The hand-rolled animation hooks are good code, and the reduced-motion handling is genuinely thoughtful. But loading React **development** builds plus **Babel-in-the-browser** on every page (index.html:16–18) is a hard no for production: you're shipping ~1.5 MB of unminified framework + a JSX compiler that blocks first paint while it transpiles three `.jsx` files client-side. Add a 1.2 MB hero-animation iframe and unsized media, and the LCP/CLS story on mobile is rough. None of it is unfixable, but `serve -l 3000 .` + CDN dev React is a "works on my laptop" setup, not a launch config.

**Stack / deployment**
- `serve -l 3000 .` (package.json:7) is a static file server — fine as a *host* (and the git log shows you're on Vercel static). The problem isn't the server; it's what it serves.
- **In-browser Babel** (`@babel/standalone`, index.html:18) transpiles `app.jsx` + page `.jsx` on every load. Render-blocking, no caching of compiled output, throws a console warning in prod, and means the page is blank until React mounts. This is the #1 technical issue.
- **React dev builds** (`react.development.js`, `react-dom.development.js`, index.html:16–17) — larger, slower, and noisy. Should be `.production.min.js` at minimum; ideally a real build step.
- No build step means no minification, no tree-shaking, no asset hashing/cache-busting, no code-splitting per page (every page reloads all of app.jsx).

**Performance red flags**
- `imagery/hero-animation.html` is **1.22 MB** (one 1.07 MB line of embedded bundle data + a 136 KB template). It's loaded in an iframe on the home hero (home.jsx:127) and "unpacks" via JS at runtime. Heavy, and the `onLoad` handler injects CSS and fires `resize` across four `setTimeout`s (home.jsx:138–159) to fight the bundler's async DOM replacement — fragile and a sign the asset isn't really meant to be embedded this way.
- **Unsized, oversized raster images.** `lumera-hero.png` 1.4 MB, `rivr-founders-team.png` 1.26 MB, `rivr-hero-medspa-patron.png` 1.13 MB. `<img>` tags have no width/height attributes anywhere → CLS risk; containers use `aspect-ratio` (good, mitigates some) but the images themselves are PNG screenshots that should be WebP/AVIF at ~1/5 the weight. `loading="lazy"` is applied in most spots (good), but the hero fallback and first paint aren't.
- **Scroll handlers everywhere.** Multiple `scroll`/`resize` listeners (useReveal, useScrollProgress, useStickyProgress per section, plus Nav, Cursor, mouse) — all rAF-throttled (good), but on a long page that's a lot of concurrent rAF loops. The 3D tilt (home.jsx:298) and sticky horizontal scroll (home.jsx:474) are the jank-prone ones on mid-tier Android.
- The "frozen rAF" fallback (app.jsx:625–662) runs a `setInterval` up to 12× to force reveal end-states in preview iframes. Harmless in real browsers but it's preview-environment scaffolding that shouldn't ship.

**Accessibility**
- **Good:** `prefers-reduced-motion` block is thorough (styles.css:1182–1190, 1321, 1404); hero animation iframe is `aria-hidden` + `tabIndex=-1` with a static fallback (home.jsx:129); modal has `role="dialog"`, `aria-modal`, focus trap-ish focus management, ESC, scroll-lock, focus restore (app.jsx:498–526); `:focus-visible` styles exist (styles.css:64); marquee has `aria-label`.
- **Broken / weak:**
  - **No skip-to-content link.** Keyboard users tab through the whole nav every page.
  - **FAQ accordions lack `aria-expanded` / `aria-controls`** (product.jsx:482, book.jsx:142). Screen-reader users don't know state.
  - **Mobile nav links removed entirely** (styles.css:1220) — not just a visual problem, it's a navigation dead-end with no accessible alternative (no hamburger, no menu button).
  - **Custom cursor with `mix-blend-mode: difference`** (styles.css:375) can reduce effective contrast unpredictably; it's hidden on touch but desktop low-vision users may lose the real cursor affordance.
  - **Contrast:** `--color-text-muted-inverse: #A3A3A3` on `#0A0A0A` (benefits/footer body) is ~ 7:1 — fine. But `#525252` muted on `#FAFAFA` for body copy is ~ 7.5:1 — fine. The thin gold `#C9A875` on white for labels (`.text-label-caps`) is ~ 2.3:1 — **fails** for the small-caps labels used as section eyebrows throughout. They're decorative-ish but they carry meaning.
  - Decorative SVG icons are `aria-hidden` (good), but the giant background "R" watermarks are also fine.

**SEO**
- Per-page `<title>` and `<meta description>` exist and are reasonable (index.html:6–7 etc.). That's the good news.
- **Missing:** Open Graph / Twitter card tags (so cold-email link previews will be blank/ugly — a real problem for an email-driven launch), `og:image`, favicon/`apple-touch-icon`, canonical URLs, `robots.txt`, `sitemap.xml`, and structured data (LocalBusiness/Organization JSON-LD would directly help a local-services business in FL).
- Client-rendered React with no SSR/prerender means crawlers see an empty `#root` until JS runs. Google can render JS but it's slower to index and link-preview bots (LinkedIn, iMessage, Outlook) generally won't — so **shared links in cold emails will preview as nothing.** For an email-launch site this is significant.

**Mobile responsiveness — real or fake?**
Mostly real. There are genuine breakpoints (900/720/600 in styles.css) and the heavy desktop interactions degrade sensibly (pipeline un-pins, horizontal scroll becomes overflow-auto — styles.css:1201–1206). But:
- The vanished mobile nav (styles.css:1220) is a real responsive *gap*, not a graceful degrade.
- The hero animation iframe at 16/9 on a narrow phone relies on runtime style-injection (home.jsx:138) to center a 1920×1080 stage — fragile; high risk of looking broken or letterboxed on real devices.

**Concrete issue list (file:line)**
- index.html:16–18 — dev React + in-browser Babel (all 4 HTML shells).
- imagery/hero-animation.html — 1.22 MB embedded-bundle iframe; home.jsx:127 embeds it; home.jsx:138–159 fragile style-injection workaround.
- home.jsx:293–295, product.jsx:75 — large PNGs, no dimensions, no WebP.
- styles.css:1220 — mobile nav links hidden, no replacement.
- product.jsx:482, book.jsx:142 — accordion missing `aria-expanded`/`aria-controls`.
- app.jsx:625–662 — preview-only rAF fallback shipped to prod.
- All HTML `<head>` — no OG/Twitter/favicon/canonical/JSON-LD; no sitemap/robots in repo.
- styles.css:375 — `mix-blend-mode` cursor contrast risk.
- `.text-label-caps` gold-on-white — sub-AA contrast for meaningful labels.

**Recommendations**
- Introduce a minimal build (Vite) or at least precompile JSX and load `react.production.min.js`. Removes Babel-in-browser and the blank-until-mount problem in one move.
- Convert screenshots to WebP/AVIF, add explicit width/height, keep `loading="lazy"`. Poster-image the hero animation on mobile (it's already wired as `.fallback-img`).
- Add OG tags + `og:image` + favicon to all four heads before the email goes out — link previews are part of the email funnel.
- Add a skip link, fix the accordions' ARIA, and give mobile a real menu.
- Add `LocalBusiness` JSON-LD (Jacksonville Beach address) for local SEO.

## Perspective 6 — Brand strategist / editorial designer

**Verdict.** This sits in an awkward middle: more considered than a Shopify/Lovable template, not yet Aesop/Goop. The bones of an editorial brand are here — restrained palette, italic-serif accents, real negative space, motion that respects the content — but it's diluted by SaaS-default reflexes (traffic-light browser chrome on every mockup, a trick cursor, the X-not-Y headline, generic stat cards) and by thin, placeholder-y content where richness should live. It reads "talented studio's tasteful template," which is *close* but not the same as "this brand has a point of view."

**Where it reads luxury / editorial / considered**
- The **marquee in italic serif** with the gold `◆` separators (styles.css:1098–1121) — genuinely elegant, feels like a fashion-house ticker rather than a logo soup.
- The **dark Benefits + Closing CTA sections** with the oversized translucent serif "R" watermark (home.jsx:416, styles.css:801) — restrained, confident, the most "brand" moments on the site.
- **Typographic hierarchy** — `text-wrap: balance` on display, `pretty` on body (styles.css:75/89), italic-serif emphasis spans inside sans headlines. That serif-inside-sans move is the signature and it works.
- The **pipeline sticky sequence** (product.jsx) — the text-message support mockup (product.jsx:166) is the single most *brand-true* artifact on the site: it shows the "real humans, text us" promise instead of asserting it.

**Where it reads Lovable-generic / Shopify-default / template**
- **Traffic-light browser chrome on every single mockup** (`<i/><i/><i/>` red-yellow-green dots — app.jsx:455, home.jsx:359, home.jsx:533, styles.css:354/698/754). This is *the* dribbble/SaaS-template cliché. Three of them stacked in the LiveDemo grid screams "product landing page," not "editorial." An editorial brand frames work like a magazine spread, not a fake macOS window.
- **The custom blend-mode cursor** (app.jsx:396) — 2021 agency-portfolio default. Gratuitous here.
